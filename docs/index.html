Inside the TWFE estimate

The static TWFE coefficient is a weighted average of every two-group, two-period comparison in the data. Here 2.4% of the weight comes from comparisons that use already-treated states as controls, which are biased when effects change over time. The weights reproduce the unweighted TWFE estimate exactly (0.0694).

How sure is the TWFE estimate?

With 50 clusters and only 21 treated, conventional cluster-robust standard errors are known to over-reject. The wild cluster restricted bootstrap and a permutation test over adoption dates ask the same question with fewer assumptions.

Specification choices

Weighting by population makes the result about the average resident; unweighted, it is about the average state. The gap shows the effect is concentrated in large states, and that the choice of comparison group matters as much as the choice of estimator.

Synthetic control: Proposition 99

In 1989 California raised its cigarette tax by 25 cents a pack. No single state is a good comparison, so a weighted blend of five is built to match California before the law. By 2000 sales were 25.7 packs per person lower than the blend. California's post/pre fit ratio ranks first of 39 states (p = 0.026).

Method

Every estimator is implemented from its paper with NumPy and SciPy.

  • TWFE: weighted least squares with unit and year effects absorbed by alternating projections; CR1 cluster-robust errors with the reghdfe/fixest small-sample correction.
  • Goodman-Bacon: closed-form weights for every timing-group pair, including always-treated units; the weighted sum reproduces TWFE to machine precision.
  • Callaway–Sant'Anna: ATT(g, t) against never- or not-yet-treated units, optional outcome-regression adjustment, influence-function errors, multiplier-bootstrap uniform bands, and simple, dynamic, cohort and calendar summaries.
  • Sun–Abraham: cohort × relative-period saturated regression, aggregated with cohort-share weights; on a balanced panel it equals Callaway–Sant'Anna with a fixed base period, which the tests check.
  • Synthetic control: simplex-constrained weights by non-negative least squares, predictor importance chosen by nested optimization of pre-period fit, and in-space placebo tests.
  • Wild cluster restricted bootstrap: vectorized over draws, exact enumeration of all sign patterns for up to 12 clusters, confidence intervals by test inversion.

Data: Cheng and Hoekstra (2013) via Cunningham's Mixtape; mpdta from the did R package; Proposition 99 data from Abadie, Diamond and Hainmueller (2010). Generated 2026-10-12. Full results in results.json.
